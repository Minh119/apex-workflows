Best Ergonomic Vertical Mice (2026): Pick by Hand Size

By Minh Tran, mechanical designer · Last reviewed Nov 2, 2026

How this guide was made: this shortlist is based on manufacturer specifications, warranty terms and patterns in published reviews and owner feedback. How we research. Prices change often, so check Amazon for current pricing.

A vertical mouse turns your hand into a handshake position, which reduces how much your forearm twists. The biggest factor in whether one feels right is fit: a mouse that's too big or too small for your hand forces you to grip harder, which defeats the point. This guide compares five popular ergonomic mice sold on Amazon US, with a simple hand-length chart to narrow the choice.

How to choose

1. Measure your hand

Measure from the crease of your wrist to the tip of your middle finger. Under about 7.5 in (19 cm) usually suits smaller vertical mice like the Logitech Lift or ProtoArc EM11; longer hands suit the MX Vertical. The chart below maps hand length to each mouse.

2. Steeper isn't always better

More vertical angles reduce forearm rotation further but take longer to get used to. Most mainstream vertical mice sit around 57–58°; Evoluent's near-vertical shape is steeper. Give any vertical mouse a week or two before deciding.

3. Battery or rechargeable

AA-powered mice (like the Lift) run for many months and you never wait on a charge; rechargeable ones (MX Vertical, ProtoArc) save on batteries but need plugging in every few weeks.

4. Left-handed?

Only some models come in a left-handed version. Logitech sells a left-handed Lift in graphite; most others here are right-handed only.

5. This isn't medical treatment

An ergonomic mouse may make you more comfortable, but it doesn't treat carpal tunnel syndrome. See a clinician for persistent pain, tingling or numbness.

The shortlist

Small to medium hands

1. Logitech Lift Vertical

The Lift puts a 57° vertical grip in a compact body with quiet clicks and a soft thumb rest. It's one of the best-selling ergonomic mice on Amazon, it runs on a single AA battery that Logitech rates at up to 24 months, and it's one of the few vertical mice sold in a left-handed version.

What we like

  • Sized for small-to-medium hands
  • Left-handed version available
  • Bluetooth or Logi Bolt receiver

Trade-offs

  • Too small for large hands
  • Uses an AA battery rather than charging

What other reviewers found

  • Tom's Hardware: praised its ergonomics, noting it's designed for smaller hands and that people with large hands are better off with the MX Vertical.

Skip it if: Your hand is longer than about 7.5 in.

Larger hands

2. Logitech MX Vertical

The larger sibling of the Lift: the same 57° angle in a bigger body, with a rechargeable battery, USB-C charging and switching between up to three computers. It's right-handed only.

What we like

  • Sized for larger hands
  • Rechargeable via USB-C
  • Switches between computers

Trade-offs

  • Right-handed only
  • Thumb buttons are awkwardly placed for some

What other reviewers found

  • PC Gamer: called it one of the most comfortable office mice, though it found the thumb buttons less ideally placed.
  • PCWorld: recommends it for people who prefer a vertical mouse or deal with RSI.

Skip it if: You have small hands or are left-handed.

Trying vertical cheaply

3. ProtoArc EM11 NL

A low-cost rechargeable vertical mouse that's become one of Amazon's top sellers in the category. It has a 58° tilt, connects to up to three devices over Bluetooth or its USB receiver, and is aimed at hands under about 7.5 in. Reviewers call it a good low-cost way to find out whether vertical suits you, with a basic sensor and a coating that may not age well.

What we like

  • Very low price
  • Rechargeable, three-device switching
  • Good fit for smaller hands

Trade-offs

  • Basic sensor (up to 2400 dpi)
  • Rubberized coating may wear

What other reviewers found

  • AppleInsider: described it as a low-cost step toward an ergonomic workspace.
  • YugaTech: liked the comfort for the price but flagged limited DPI options, a learning curve and a coating that may not age well.

Skip it if: You have large hands or want a precise sensor for design work.

Steepest, most vertical grip

4. Evoluent VerticalMouse 4

Evoluent was one of the first vertical mouse makers, and the VM4 has a steeper, near-vertical shape than the 57° Logitech models, which reduces forearm rotation further. It comes in small and regular sizes, wired or wireless, with programmable buttons. We didn't find a recent independent review, and it takes longer to adapt to than gentler designs.

What we like

  • Steepest grip in this group
  • Size options
  • Programmable buttons

Trade-offs

  • Longer adjustment period
  • Pricey for its dated design

Skip it if: You want something that feels familiar on day one.

Not ready for vertical

5. Logitech MX Master 3S

Not a vertical mouse, but a sculpted shape that supports your palm, with a fast scroll wheel, a thumb wheel and quiet clicks. A sensible step if a full vertical grip feels too unfamiliar, though it reduces forearm twisting less than the mice above.

  • Horizontal thumb scroll wheel
  • Quiet clicks

Trade-offs

FAQ

Is a vertical mouse good for wrist pain?

Many people find a vertical grip more comfortable because it reduces forearm twisting, but it isn't a treatment. If you have ongoing pain, tingling or numbness, see a clinician.

How long does it take to get used to a vertical mouse?

Usually a few days to two weeks. Precision feels off at first; keep the pointer speed a little lower until your hand adapts.

Do vertical mice work for left-handed people?

Only some. The Logitech Lift has a left-handed version; most others here are right-handed only, so check before buying.
