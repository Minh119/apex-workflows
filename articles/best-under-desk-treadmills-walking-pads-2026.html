Best Under-Desk Treadmills & Walking Pads (2026): Size, Speed & Desk Height

A walking pad turns standing-desk time into gentle movement. For desk use, what matters is a speed range suited to walking while typing, a weight rating with some headroom, a footprint you can store, and one thing most listings skip: the deck adds 4–5 in to your height, so your standing desk has to go that much higher. We compared three popular pads on those numbers and worked out the desk height you'll need.

How to choose

1. Check your desk can go high enough

Standing on a pad raises you by its deck height, about 4–5 in on popular models. Your standing desk has to reach your standing elbow height plus that deck. The table below shows the height you'll need; our calculator has a walking-pad option.

2. Walking speed is slow

Most people type comfortably at about 1–2 mph. You don't need running speeds for desk use; a top speed around 3.7–4 mph is plenty.

3. Weight rating and belt size

Leave headroom below the user weight limit, and check that the belt is long and wide enough for your stride (most desk pads are about 16 in wide).

4. Noise and floors

Claimed noise figures are often optimistic: one reviewer measured a pad rated under 45 dB at about 57 dB. Put a mat underneath to cut vibration, especially in apartments or on calls.

5. Storage

Fold-in-half pads like WalkingPad's slide under a sofa or bed; flat pads need a wall or closet to lean against.

FAQ

How fast should I walk at a standing desk?

Most people can type and take calls comfortably around 1–2 mph. Start slow; you can speed up for reading or meetings.

Do I need a special standing desk for a walking pad?

You need one that goes high enough. A walking pad adds about 4–5 in to your height, so your desk must reach your standing elbow height plus the deck. See the table above.

Are walking pads loud?

Quieter than running treadmills, but often louder than advertised. A mat underneath helps with vibration, especially in apartments.
