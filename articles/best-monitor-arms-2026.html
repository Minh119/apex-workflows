A monitor arm lets you put the top of your screen at eye level, pull it closer or push it away, and free up desk space. The most important check is fit: your monitor's weight has to fall within the arm's rated range, and its mounting holes have to match. Here are five arms for different setups.

How to choose

Weigh your monitor without its stand

Look up the monitor's weight without the stand on its spec sheet. It must fall inside the arm's range: gas-spring arms have a minimum as well as a maximum, so a very light screen can drift up just as a heavy one sags.

Check the VESA pattern

Most arms fit 75 × 75 mm and 100 × 100 mm hole patterns. Some monitors (including certain Apple and curved models) need an adapter, so check before you order.

Know your target height

Our calculator estimates your seated eye height. Make sure the arm can lift the top of your screen to that height from your desk surface.

Check your desk edge

Clamp mounts need clearance behind the desk and a solid edge. Thin, glass or particleboard desks may need a reinforcement plate. A grommet mount uses an existing cable hole instead.

Curved and ultrawide screens need more

Large curved monitors are heavy and deep, which reduces an arm's effective capacity. Choose an arm rated for the size and weight, like the Ergotron HX.

FAQ

How high should my monitor be?

The top of the screen should sit at or slightly below eye level, about an arm's length away. Our calculator estimates your seated eye height from your body height.

Will a monitor arm damage my desk?

On a solid desk, no. Thin, glass or particleboard desktops can crack or dent under a clamp; a steel reinforcement plate spreads the load.

My monitor has no VESA holes. Can I still use an arm?

Often yes, with a VESA adapter bracket made for your monitor size. Check that the adapter's weight rating covers your monitor.

Do monitor arms work on standing desks?

Yes. Leave enough cable slack for the full height range, and count the monitor and arm weight toward your desk's lift capacity.
