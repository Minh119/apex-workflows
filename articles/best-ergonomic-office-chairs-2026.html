Every chair on Amazon is called "ergonomic". What actually decides whether a chair works for you is fit: whether the seat goes low enough for your feet to rest flat, whether the seat depth suits your thigh length, and whether the lumbar support lines up with your lower back. After that come load rating and warranty, which tell you how long the chair is built to last. We compared three popular chairs on those numbers, using manufacturer specs and independent reviews, and worked out which heights each one fits.

How to choose

1. Seat height: the lowest setting matters most

With your feet flat, your thighs should be roughly level. Our calculator and desk height chart give your ideal seat height. Shorter people should check a chair's lowest seat height: many "big and tall" chairs start around 18–19 in, which leaves anyone under about 5'10" with dangling feet unless they add a footrest.

2. Seat depth

You want 2–3 finger-widths between the front edge of the seat and the back of your knees. Adjustable seat depth (a sliding seat) helps both shorter and taller people; a fixed deep seat is the most common fit problem for shorter users.

3. Lumbar support you can move

Lower backs sit at different heights. Height-adjustable lumbar support fits more people than a fixed bump; adjustable depth (how far it pushes) is a bonus.

4. Armrests

Armrests should let your shoulders relax with elbows at desk height. If they can't drop low enough or slide out of the way, they'll push you away from the desk.

5. Load rating and warranty

A higher load rating and a longer warranty usually mean heavier-duty parts. Warranties in this group range from 3 years to Steelcase's limited lifetime coverage.

6. Mesh vs. foam

Mesh stays cooler in warm rooms; foam spreads pressure more evenly and feels softer. Neither is better for everyone, so check the return policy in case the seat doesn't suit you.

FAQ

What seat height do I need?

With your feet flat, your thighs should be roughly level. Our calculator gives a starting point based on your height.

My desk is too high for my chair. What should I do?

Raise the chair until your elbows line up with the desk, then support your feet with a footrest, or lower your keyboard with a tray. See our footrest and keyboard tray guide.

Is mesh or foam better?

Mesh stays cooler in warm rooms; foam spreads pressure more evenly and feels softer. Neither is better for everyone.

Why does a chair's lowest seat height matter?

If the seat can't go low enough, your feet dangle and the front edge presses into the back of your thighs. Compare the chair's lowest seat height with your ideal seat height before buying, or plan on a footrest.
