Best Footrests & Keyboard Trays (2026): Fix a Desk That's Too High

By Minh Tran, mechanical designer · Last reviewed Nov 2, 2026

How this guide was made: this shortlist is based on manufacturer specifications, warranty terms and patterns in published reviews and owner feedback. How we research. Prices change often, so check Amazon for current pricing.

Standard fixed desks are about 29–30 inches tall. That's close to right for people around 6 ft, and too high for almost everyone shorter: you end up typing with raised shoulders, or raising your chair until your feet dangle. There are two fixes: raise your chair and support your feet with a footrest, or lower your keyboard with an under-desk tray. This guide shows how tall a footrest you need for your height, and compares five popular options sold on Amazon US.

How to choose

1. Find out how big the gap is

Use our calculator or desk height chart to get your ideal sitting desk height, and compare it with your desk. The table below shows the footrest height that gap translates into.

2. Footrest: raise the chair, then support your feet

Set your chair so your elbows line up with the desk, then check the gap between the floor and your feet. That's the footrest height you need. Adjustable footrests make it easy to dial in; fixed-height ones only work if they happen to match.

3. Keyboard tray: lower the keyboard instead

A tray lowers your keyboard and mouse below the desktop so you can keep your chair at a normal height. It works well when several people share a desk or when your chair can't go high enough. Most trays only drop a few inches, so for very big gaps you may need both a tray and a footrest.

4. Check your desk before buying a tray

Clamp-on trays need a front edge they can grip; check the desk thickness range on the listing. Track-style drawers screw into the underside, so they need a solid wood or particleboard desk, enough clear depth, and no crossbar in the way (a common clash on standing desks).

How tall a footrest you need

At a standard 29.5 in desk, raise your chair until your elbows line up with the desktop. The footrest then needs to make up the difference between your new seat height and your ideal one, which is the same as the gap between the desk and your ideal sitting desk height (from our desk height chart, regular shoes).

FAQ

Do I need a footrest?

If your feet don't rest flat on the floor once your chair is high enough for your elbows to reach the desk, yes. Dangling feet put pressure on the back of your thighs.

How high should a footrest be?

As high as the gap between the floor and your feet when your thighs are level and your elbows line up with the desk. At a standard desk that's roughly the difference between 29.5 in and your ideal sitting desk height; see the table above.

Footrest or keyboard tray: which is better?

A footrest is cheaper and needs no installation. A keyboard tray is better when the desk is shared, when your chair can't go high enough, or when you'd rather keep your feet on the floor.

Is a keyboard tray better than a standing desk?

If your only problem is a desk that's a few inches too high, a tray is a cheap fix. If you also want to stand, a height-adjustable desk solves both. See our standing desk guide.
