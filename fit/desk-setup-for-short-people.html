Desk & Chair Setup for Short People (4'10" to 5'4")

By Minh Tran, mechanical designer · Last reviewed Nov 2, 2026

A standard desk suits someone around 6 ft tall, and many office chairs don't go low enough for short legs. If you're shorter, a standard desk leaves you with raised shoulders or dangling feet. Here are your numbers, and the chairs and desks we've checked that actually go low enough.

Your numbers, and how far off a standard desk is

A standard desk is about 29.5 in tall, sized for people around 6 ft. The shorter you are, the bigger the gap between that and your ideal sitting desk height:

Two ways to fix a standard desk

1. Raise the chair, then support your feet

Raise your chair until your elbows line up with the desktop. Your feet will then dangle, so a footrest makes up the difference, roughly the gap in the table above. At 5'0" that's about 5.1 in, so check a footrest's height before you buy; adjustable ones cover the widest range.

2. Lower the keyboard instead

An under-desk keyboard tray drops your keyboard and mouse below the desktop, so you can keep your chair low and your feet on the floor. Both options are compared in our footrest and keyboard tray guide, including how tall a footrest you need for your height.

FAQ

What desk height do I need if I'm 5'0"?

About 24.4 in for sitting and 38.8 in for standing, in regular shoes. A standard 29.5 in desk is about 5 in too high.

What's the best chair height for a short person?

Low enough that your feet rest flat with thighs level: about 15.7 in at 5'0" and 16.2 in at 5'2". If your chair doesn't go that low, use a footrest.

Footrest or keyboard tray?

A footrest keeps your keyboard on the desk and works with any chair; a keyboard tray lets you keep your chair low. If the gap is large (3 in or more), many people combine a slightly raised chair with a footrest.
